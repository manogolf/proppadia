The method. Root-Unit stakes f = f₀ / √b — your even-money unit divided by the square root of the net fractional odds b (decimal price − 1). It follows from Kelly's edge-over-odds once you assume the edge available at any price tracks e = e₀√b (Abrams' LOCO-EG curve, fit to Buchdahl's unit-z data): then f = e/b = e₀√b / b = f₀/√b. So you size off the price alone — favorites get a bigger multiple, longshots a smaller one, all earning the same expected growth if your edge follows the curve. The optional edge inputs don't change the Root-Unit stake; they drive the Kelly comparison and show the even-money-equivalent edge e₀ = edge/√b your bet implies, so you can see when a specific edge runs ahead of or behind your baseline. Works for any Kelly fraction. Abrams notes it adds little on ~50/50 spread and total markets, where both sides sit near −110 and b barely moves. This is a sizing tool, not betting or financial advice.
